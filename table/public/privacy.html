Table rules

Chips are for the game. They are not money, and nothing here can be cashed out. A table is private: you need its ID and password. The name beside a seat is the one that player chose. You can report a name, a camera, or a microphone, and you can block a seat. We can close a table or remove a player who is making it unusable.

Your account

Opening a table needs a Google or Apple sign-in. Joining a friend’s table does not. You can delete the account from Settings. Deleting it signs you out and closes every table you opened. The steps, and what is removed or kept, are on the account page.

Privacy

If you sign in, we keep the email and name your Google or Apple account gives us, so we can recognize you the next time you open a table. At a table we keep the name you chose, the chips, and the moves, so the game can continue. Camera and microphone stay off until you turn them on, and they are only for the people at that table. A report includes the table, the seat, and the reason you picked. A note to support is used to answer you. A banner ad may appear at the table, and the ad network can tell that it was shown. We may record that a table was opened or a hand was played, tied to that table, so we can see when the game breaks. We don’t sell this information.

Accessibility statement

If something in the app gets in the way, tell us what you were trying to do. We will answer, and when the screen itself cannot do it, we will arrange another way to get the same thing done. The standard we are working toward is Israeli Standard 5568, which follows WCAG 2.0 at level AA. A formal audit is not finished, so this is not a claim that every screen already meets that level. A few places we already know are harder: the poker and backgammon boards are drawn as a game, so a screen reader may not announce every chip or checker; camera and microphone have no text equivalent; banner ads come from another company.

To ask for an adjustment, or to tell us something isn’t usable, write to support@loochka.com.
